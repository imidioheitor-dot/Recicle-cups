No trucks, no third-party processor, no shipping a bale of plastic across the country and hoping. The bottle and the finished object are never more than one building apart.

04 — The machine

Built for
$968

A commercial filament extruder costs $2,945 — three times our entire budget. So we designed our own around a pultrusion head instead of a screw, and came in under a thousand dollars with change to spare.

Tap any marker on the drawing.

Irish Comeback · Unit 01 Rev 1.0 — Scale 1:8

Marker 01

Shredder drum

Twelve hardened blades on a 20 mm hex shaft, geared 60:1 down to roughly 70 rpm. Low speed, very high torque — the opposite of a blender. It handles caps and HDPE, never PET bottles.

Blade set $110 · shaft $24 · reducer $145

Marker 02

Dehydrator

A $45 food dehydrator, and the most important component in the build. Four hours at 65 °C drives moisture out of the PET before it ever sees heat. Skip it and every spool you make snaps in your hands.

65 °C · 4 h · desiccant storage after

Marker 03

Pultrusion barrel

Two heater bands hold 245 °C. A PET ribbon enters flat and leaves round. There is no screw and no melt reservoir — the plastic is pulled, not pushed, which is precisely why this fits a student budget.

245 °C · output 1.75 ± 0.05 mm

Marker 04

Spooler

Closed-loop pull rate. The winder speed is the diameter control: pull faster and the filament thins, slower and it swells. Every spool gets sampled at ten points before it is cleared.

Stepper-driven · tension compensated

Marker 05

Drive

A 1.5 HP surplus motor behind a worm reducer, with an E-stop wired in series on the primary side. Nothing in this build spins without a way to make it stop immediately.

1.5 HP · 110 V · E-stop mandatory

05 — Built

Sixty-four
kilos of
evidence

Ninety-one parts. Two lines sharing one bench. A drum that turns at seventy revolutions a minute and a barrel that holds two hundred and forty-five degrees without drifting.

Laid out flat, it is a modest pile of steel and a few printed brackets. Assembled, it closes a loop that currently ends in a landfill.
